## Section 2: Core Business Analysis Concepts (LinkedIn Learning)

1. **Problem, Opportunity, & Vision Statements:**
   * **Problem Statement:** Identifies current business pain points and costs.
   * **Opportunity Statement:** Defines potential solutions or technological upgrades.
   * **Vision Statement:** Summarizes the project's high-level goal and business value.

2. **Current State vs. Future State Analysis:**
   * **As-Is (Current State):** Documents existing workflows to pinpoint bottlenecks.
   * **To-Be (Future State):** Designs optimized workflows post-implementation.
   * **Gap Analysis:** Identifies requirements needed to move from As-Is to To-Be.

3. **The Requirements Value Chain:**
   Business Goal -> User Requirements -> System Design -> Code / Implementation
   *(Ensures every feature directly traces back to a business goal.)*

4. **Structured Requirements Information:**
   * Categorizing requirements into Business, User, Functional, and Non-Functional.

## Section 3: Tool Notes Templates (4-Line Structure)

### 🔹 [Tool 1: Jira]
* **What it is:** An industry-standard project management and issue-tracking platform.
* **Why it is used:** To manage agile workflows, track sprint progress, and assign tasks.
* **Common use cases:** Managing backlogs, writing user stories, and tracking bug fixes.
* **Example:** Creating a user story ticket with clear acceptance criteria for developers.

### 🔹 [Tool 2: Draw.io / Lucid chart]
* **What it is:** A web-based diagramming and flowcharting application.
* **Why it is used:** To visually communicate business processes and system logic.
* **Common use cases:** Drawing BPMN process maps, swim lane diagrams, and architecture flows.
* **Example:** Mapping out an As-Is customer checkout process to highlight manual delay steps.

### 🔹 [Tool 3: Confluence]
* **What it is:** A collaborative team workspace and knowledge management tool.
* **Why it is used:** To centralize project documentation, specifications, and meeting notes.
* **Common use cases:** Writing Business Requirement Documents (BRDs) and product specs.
* **Example:** Documenting sprint goals, API specifications, and UAT test plans.

### 🔹 [Tool 4: SQL]
* **What it is:** Structured Query Language used for managing relational databases.
* **Why it is used:** To extract, manipulate, and analyse underlying data.
* **Common use cases:** Verifying data accuracy between front-end UI and backend database.
* **Example:** Running `SELECT` queries with `JOIN`s to validate submitted student timesheet records.
